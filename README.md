<h1><strong>26 Amazingly Cheap Places You Should Travel to in 2026</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The cheapest places to travel in 2026 aren&rsquo;t all in one corner of the map.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">They stretch from Vietnamese lantern towns to Balkan lakes, Moroccan medinas, and a few U.S. cities where the best attractions cost nothing.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guide pulls together 26 of them, using cost data published between June and September 2026, plus the State Department&rsquo;s latest safety ratings, so you can see both the price tag and the risk level before you book.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A quick reality check on timing: we&rsquo;re in late October, so only a couple of months of 2026 remain.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Most picks below work just as well for early 2027, but confirm current prices before you commit.</span></p>
<h2><strong>How We Picked These 26 Places</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Each destination had to clear three bars:</span></p>
<ol>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Low daily costs on the ground,</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> covering beds, meals, and local transport.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>A sensible safety record.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Every international pick lists its current U.S. State Department advisory level, checked on October 21, 2026.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Real things to do.</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Cheap is no fun if there&rsquo;s nothing worth seeing.</span></li>
</ol>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Two cost notes before the list. First, a cheap destination can still be a pricey trip if the flight eats your budget.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">&nbsp;Going&rsquo;s 2026 budget travel guide makes the same point and says shoulder-season timing can cut airfare by 20 to 40 percent. Second, exchange rates matter.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Japan looks like a bargain this year mainly because of a weak yen.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Southeast Asia: Still the Budget Benchmark</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>1. Hoi An, Vietnam</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hoi An is the kind of town where lanterns light up the old quarter at dusk and nobody asks you to spend much.</span><a href="https://www.getyourguide.com/explorer/travel-inspiration/cheapest-travel-destinations/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">&nbsp;</span></a></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GetYourGuide&rsquo;s June 2026 price snapshot lists coffee at about $1.30, a beer at about $0.87, and dinner at about $4.30.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Its monthly Lantern Festival is a highlight, and plenty of walking-tour options keep sightseeing cheap.</span></p>
<p><strong>Best time to go:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Going suggests February to April or August to October, which dodge the Lunar New Year price spike.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br /></span> <strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1, exercise normal precautions.</span></p>
<h3><strong>2. Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ho Chi Minh City brings big-city energy without big-city prices. Going puts Vietnam at $30 to $50 a day for backpackers and points to the Cu Chi Tunnels and the floating markets of the Mekong Delta as top draws.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hostelworld adds that war museums here cost only a dollar or two, and a sleeper bunk on the north-south Reunification Express train can run $25 to $30 if booked ahead.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>3. Siem Reap, Cambodia</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Angkor Wat is the splurge, and everything else is a bargain. GetYourGuide lists dinner at about $8, beer at about $1, and a local transit ride at about $0.37.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hostelworld puts a one-day Angkor pass near $37 and a three-day pass around $62, so a multi-day pass gives you better value per day.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GetYourGuide also suggests arriving before 5 a.m. for a good sunrise spot.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2, exercise increased caution (updated September 15, 2026).</span></p>
<h3><strong>4. Chiang Mai, Thailand</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Thailand&rsquo;s islands cost far more than its north. Going says Phuket and Koh Samui run 40 to 60 percent pricier than Chiang Mai, Pai, or Chiang Rai, and it puts a typical budget at $25 to $50 a day.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One new wrinkle for 2026: Going notes a 300-baht tourist entry fee (about $9) for air arrivals.</span></p>
<p><strong>Best time to go:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> May to June or September to October, per Going.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br /></span><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>5. Yogyakarta, Indonesia</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Skip the Bali markup and head to Java. GetYourGuide lists coffee at about $0.70, dinner at about $3.50, and a transit ticket at about $0.25.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The city is the base for Borobudur and Prambanan, and you can catch the Ramayana Ballet at Prambanan at night.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Going says hostel beds in Yogyakarta, Lombok, and Flores run about $6 to $13.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2.</span></p>
<h2><strong>South and East Asia: Big Contrasts</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>6. India</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">India is the classic shoestring country. Hostelworld&rsquo;s guide, updated September 17, 2026, says you can get by on under $25 a day with local food and transport.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dorm beds run about $4 to $6, and a street-food meal costs $1 to $3. Budget extra for big-ticket sights, since the Taj Mahal costs about $16 for foreign visitors.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2.</span></p>
<h3><strong>7. Nepal</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nepal gives you Himalayan views for $25 to $30 a day, according to Hostelworld.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dorm beds in Thamel and Pokhara run $5 to $10, and a plate of dal bhat costs $1.50 to $3. Independent trekkers should plan on roughly $50 to $60 for permits on popular routes, and shorter treks like Poon Hill cost less.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2, with natural disasters flagged as the main risk.</span></p>
<h3><strong>8. Ella, Sri Lanka</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ella is a hill town wrapped in tea plantations, and GetYourGuide lists dinner at about $5, a beer at about $2, and a transit fare at about $0.15.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Little Adam&rsquo;s Peak and the Nine Arches Bridge are free to visit. Second-class train tickets keep the scenic ride cheap.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2.</span></p>
<h3><strong>9. Japan</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Japan isn&rsquo;t cheap by Vietnam standards, but 2026 is unusually kind to dollar holders. Going reports the yen trading around 161 to 164 per dollar, close to its weakest level since 1986, and estimates a 25 to 30 percent discount for American visitors.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Budget travelers can expect $80 to $120 a day, flights excluded. Crowds are heavy, so book beds and popular experiences early.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Going suggests January to February (outside New Year week) or June for lower prices.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Europe: Bargains Without the Long Flight Home</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>10. Albania</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Albania has beaches, mountains, and Ottoman-era towns at prices that make Greece and Italy look steep.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Going puts daily costs at $30 to $55, with guesthouse rooms at $15 to $30 and meals at $5 to $10.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Top stops include the Riviera around Himara and Dh&euml;rmi, the UNESCO-listed old town of Berat, and the Valbona-to-Theth hike.</span></p>
<p><strong>Best time to go:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> May to June or September, before July and August push coastal prices up.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br /></span><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2, with crime flagged (updated August 26, 2026).</span></p>
<h3><strong>11. Bulgaria</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hostelworld calls Bulgaria one of Europe&rsquo;s cheapest countries and says backpackers can get by on under $40 a day.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dorm beds run under $15, a hearty tavern meal costs roughly $5 to $8, and a ride on Sofia&rsquo;s public transit costs under $1.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rila Monastery is free to enter, and ski towns like Bansko cost far less than Alpine resorts.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>12. Brașov, Romania</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Castles, cobblestones, and a mountain backdrop for about $50 a day, per Going. A hostel bed runs $14 to $20 and sit-down meals cost $4 to $17.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The train from Bucharest takes under four hours and costs 5 to 15 euros. Bran Castle, the one people call Dracula&rsquo;s, makes an easy day trip.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>13. Krak&oacute;w, Poland</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Krak&oacute;w is a UNESCO old town with prices well below Western Europe. Going says backpackers manage $31 to $50 a day in Poland and puts Krak&oacute;w at roughly $40 a day.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It adds that Krak&oacute;w and Wrocław both undercut Warsaw. If you plan to hop between Central European cities by coach, this</span><a href="https://adorable-watermelon-123s2bk.mystrikingly.com/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">FlixBus bus service review</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is a useful read before you book, and FlixBus lists Poland among its European destinations.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>14. Portugal</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Portugal is the priciest pick in this group, but it&rsquo;s the best value in Western Europe. Going puts it at $55 to $70 a day, and says Porto undercuts Lisbon on both hotels and food.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Spring and late fall dodge the summer rush. FlixBus also lists Portugal among its European destinations, so a coach between cities is worth pricing out.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>15. Ohrid, North Macedonia</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lake Ohrid and its UNESCO old town draw far fewer visitors than Europe&rsquo;s big names. GetYourGuide lists coffee at about $1.50, a beer at about $1.10, and dinner at about $10.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Its tip is smart: skip the touristy lakeside restaurants and try family-run bakeries for burek and tavče gravče.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 1.</span></p>
<h3><strong>16. Chișinău, Moldova</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Moldova is famously the least-visited country in Europe, which keeps the capital calm and cheap.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GetYourGuide lists dinner at about $10, a beer at about $1.60, and a transit ride at about $0.35.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Parks and Soviet-era monuments cost nothing to explore, and wine tastings are a local specialty.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2. Read the full advisory before planning any day trips outside the capital.</span></p>
<h3><strong>17. Istanbul, T&uuml;rkiye</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Istanbul stretches a budget a long way. Hostelworld lists dorm beds at $10 to $15, a d&ouml;ner sandwich at about $1 to $2, and a transit ride on the Istanbulkart at about $0.50 to $1, though those lira prices date from late 2025.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Wandering the Grand Bazaar is free, and a Bosphorus ferry costs about the same as any local transit ride.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2.</span></p>
<h3><strong>18. Antalya, T&uuml;rkiye</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For a beach break, Antalya beats most resort towns on price. GetYourGuide lists coffee at about $0.50, dinner at about $9.50, and a transit ticket at about $0.64.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A tram links the center, the beaches, and the airport, and the old town (Kalei&ccedil;i) is fully walkable.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ancient sites like Aspendos, Perge, and Hadrian&rsquo;s Gate sit nearby. Lira prices move quickly, so treat these as a guide, not a quote.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2.</span></p>
<h2><strong>North Africa and the Andes</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>19. Morocco</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Morocco offers souks, mountains, and desert for modest money. Hostelworld says dorm beds run $8 to $10, a tagine or couscous plate costs $2 to $4, and a comfortable CTM or Supratours bus ride of four to five hours costs about $8 to $10.&nbsp;</span></p>

<h3><strong>20. Cusco, Peru</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cusco is the gateway to Machu Picchu, and the city itself is easy on the wallet. GetYourGuide lists coffee at about $2, a beer at about $2.35, and a transit ride at about $0.30, but restaurant dinners run higher at about $19.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The city sits near 3,400 meters (11,200 feet), so take it slowly on day one and drink coca tea.</span></p>

<h3><strong>21. Bolivia</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hostelworld calls Bolivia arguably the cheapest country in South America. Dorm beds run $6 to $10, and a set lunch (almuerzo) costs about $3 to $4.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The bus from La Paz to Copacabana often costs under $5. A three-day Uyuni Salt Flats tour runs around $120 to $150, food and lodging included.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Level 2, with crime and unrest flagged (updated August 18, 2026).</span></p>
<h3><strong>22. Oaxaca, Mexico</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Most budget travelers think Canc&uacute;n, but Going says the real savings sit inland. It puts Oaxaca at $40 to $60 a day and says its food scene rivals Mexico City&rsquo;s at about half the price.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Prices spike around D&iacute;a de Muertos at the end of October and start of November, so February to April is the cheaper window.</span></p>
<p><strong>Safety check:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Mexico is Level 2 overall (updated May 29, 2026). Advisories can differ by region, so read the state-by-state details first.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The United States: Cheap Trips Without a Passport</strong></h2>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cabins for groups can come in under $200 a night, and January and February (outside holiday weeks) are the cheapest months.</span></p>
<h3><strong>25. Chicago, Illinois</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Going lists Chicago among the cities routinely ranked cheapest in the U.S., because of free or low-cost attractions and affordable lodging.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That makes it a good pick for a big-city weekend when you want skyline views without a skyline-sized bill.</span></p>
